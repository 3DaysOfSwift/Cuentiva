# Your turn and Match Pairs

Optional post-completion activities, available to paid readers for completed books. The first completion of a calendar day offers a weekly stamp/streak animation before the invitation. Existing streak qualification (sentence encounters) remains unchanged; no new book completion or vocabulary mastery is granted for replay activities. Reduce Motion disables the entrance animation. Completed shelf menus expose Read full book, Your turn and Match pairs, both visibly and on long press.

Your turn uses the full existing Spanish text with audio off. A 60–180 words/minute guide can be started, paused or restarted; tapping a sentence toggles its English translation. The reader chooses when they are finished. Statistics distinguish total tokens, distinct written forms and approximate lemma families. New encounters capture word-form history before the book's first recorded sentence. Historical installs without complete word-form history explicitly report that the before-book split is unavailable rather than guessing. Existing completed counts and legacy JSON remain compatible.

Match Pairs has a three-second countdown, a monotonic 30-second timer and an untimed option. Five pairs are displayed at once; each distinct word enters the deck exactly once. Identical English meanings never compete on one board. Leaving/backgrounding a round interrupts it without reward; replays start fresh. The result shows accuracy, missed forms and a saved personal best. One doubloon per book is saved atomically after the first finished round with at least one correct match, regardless of timed/untimed mode. Zero matches and abandoned rounds grant nothing. Save failures expose a retry. Coins cannot be spent yet.

The first two bundled books (cafe, garden) contain explicit full word-form glossaries, authored and manually checked against the source sentences during this implementation. Short phrases are used for grammatical words and inflected forms. This is not external native-speaker certification. Remaining books deliberately show an unavailable message until their full glossaries are authored; partial decks are not presented as complete. A glossary must cover every indexed word to enable play.
