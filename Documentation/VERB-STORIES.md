# Verbs through stories

Three original books: ser, estar, querer. The focus is the six present-indicative forms, not every tense or mood. Includes vosotros (Spain); usted/ustedes share the third-person forms. Regional voseo is not covered in this first set. Other tenses may occur naturally in the narrative. Each focus form appears in both the guided half and the equally long continuation. No conjugation chart is shown. Levels are approximate; native-speaker review pending.

## Ser: The letter

Identity, relationships, and roles. Six present-indicative forms; vosotros is used in Spain.

### Guided lesson

Ana encuentra una carta y descubre que ser puede cambiar la historia de una familia.

Ana finds a letter and discovers that the verb ser can change a family’s story.

Su hermano dice: «Soy tu hermano, aunque esta carta diga otra cosa».

Her brother says, “I am your brother, even if this letter says something else.”

Ana responde: «Eres mi hermano, pero necesito entender quién es esta mujer».

Ana replies, “You are my brother, but I need to understand who this woman is.”

Su madre entra y dice: «Es mi hermana; es vuestra tía».

Their mother comes in and says, “She is my sister; she is your aunt.”

«Somos una familia, pero nunca hablamos de ella», protesta Ana.

“We are a family, but we never talk about her,” Ana protests.

«Sois mis hijos y merecéis una explicación», responde su madre.

“You are my children and you deserve an explanation,” their mother replies.

«Estas cartas son suyas; son parte de una historia difícil».

“These letters are hers; they are part of a difficult story.”

Ana levanta la voz: «Soy su sobrina y no sé ni su nombre».

Ana raises her voice: “I am her niece and I do not even know her name.”

Su hermano añade: «Eres nuestra madre; queremos escucharte».

Her brother adds, “You are our mother; we want to listen to you.”

«Es una conversación que he evitado demasiado tiempo», admite ella.

“It is a conversation I have avoided for too long,” she admits.

Ana deja la carta sobre la mesa: «Somos capaces de escuchar algo difícil».

Ana puts the letter on the table: “We are capable of hearing something difficult.”

La madre se sienta y, por primera vez, abre la caja de cartas.

Their mother sits down and, for the first time, opens the box of letters.

### Full-reader continuation

«Soy la hermana mayor, pero eso no significa que siempre tenga razón», empieza.

“I am the older sister, but that does not mean I am always right,” she begins.

Ana contesta: «Eres humana, mamá; no tienes que ser perfecta».

Ana replies, “You are human, Mum; you do not have to be perfect.”

«Su nombre es Clara y es una persona muy importante para mí».

“Her name is Clara and she is a very important person to me.”

El hermano mira las fotos: «Somos muy parecidos cuando sonreímos».

Her brother looks at the photographs: “We look very similar when we smile.”

Su madre asiente: «Sois sus sobrinos, aunque todavía no os conozca».

Their mother nods: “You are her niece and nephew, even though she does not know you yet.”

«Estas son las fotos que quería enviarle y nunca envié».

“These are the photographs I wanted to send her and never sent.”

Ana pregunta si pueden escribir juntos una carta nueva.

Ana asks whether they can write a new letter together.

Empieza así: «Soy Ana, tu sobrina, y quiero conocerte».

It begins like this: “I am Ana, your niece, and I want to meet you.”

Su hermano añade: «Somos dos, y tenemos muchas preguntas».

Her brother adds, “There are two of us, and we have lots of questions.”

La madre escribe: «Eres mi hermana; esa parte de nuestra historia sigue aquí».

Their mother writes, “You are my sister; that part of our story is still here.”

Ahora ser les sirve para nombrar sus vínculos, no para decidir quién tiene la culpa.

Now ser helps them name their relationships, rather than decide who is to blame.

La carta no arregla todo, pero es una puerta que por fin se abre.

The letter does not fix everything, but it is a door that finally opens.

## Estar: Behind the curtain

Location and how someone feels or is doing. Six present-indicative forms; vosotros is used in Spain.

### Guided lesson

Antes de la función, estar cuenta dónde está cada persona y cómo se siente.

Before the performance, estar tells us where each person is and how they feel.

«Estoy detrás del telón y estoy muy nerviosa», dice Inés por teléfono.

“I am behind the curtain and I am very nervous,” Inés says on the phone.

Su hermano pregunta: «¿Estás sola o estás con los demás?».

Her brother asks, “Are you alone or are you with the others?”

«La directora está aquí, pero mi vestido no está en su sitio».

“The director is here, but my dress is not in its place.”

Dos actores llegan corriendo: «Estamos listos, pero estamos preocupados por ti».

Two actors come running: “We are ready, but we are worried about you.”

La directora pregunta: «¿Estáis seguros de que habéis mirado en todas las cajas?».

The director asks, “Are you sure you have looked in all the boxes?”

«Los vestidos están allí, pero las cajas están cerradas», responde un actor.

“The dresses are over there, but the boxes are closed,” an actor replies.

Inés grita: «¡Estoy cansada de buscar y el público ya está dentro!».

Inés shouts, “I am tired of searching and the audience is already inside!”

La directora baja la voz: «Estás asustada; vamos a buscar juntas».

The director lowers her voice: “You are frightened; let us look together.”

«Estamos contigo», repiten los actores mientras apartan las sillas.

“We are with you,” the actors repeat as they move the chairs aside.

Entonces Inés ve una esquina roja debajo de una mesa.

Then Inés sees a red corner beneath a table.

El vestido está allí, junto a una nota que no reconoce.

The dress is there, beside a note she does not recognize.

### Full-reader continuation

«Estoy aquí para verte», dice la nota de su hermano.

“I am here to see you,” says the note from her brother.

Inés vuelve a llamarlo: «¿Estás en el teatro de verdad?».

Inés calls him again: “Are you really in the theater?”

«Sí, mi asiento está en la primera fila».

“Yes, my seat is in the front row.”

Inés mira a sus compañeros: «Estamos a tiempo; todavía podemos salir».

Inés looks at her fellow actors: “We are in time; we can still go on.”

La directora sonríe: «Estáis preparados y yo estoy a vuestro lado».

The director smiles: “You are ready and I am by your side.”

Las luces están encendidas y las puertas están cerradas.

The lights are on and the doors are closed.

«Estoy nerviosa todavía», admite Inés mientras se pone el vestido.

“I am still nervous,” Inés admits as she puts on the dress.

«Estás aquí, y eso es suficiente para empezar», responde su amiga.

“You are here, and that is enough to begin,” her friend replies.

Cuando se abre el telón, Inés ve que su hermano está sonriendo.

When the curtain opens, Inés sees that her brother is smiling.

«Estamos juntos», piensa, aunque él esté entre el público.

“We are together,” she thinks, even though he is in the audience.

Estar le ha servido para pedir ayuda, encontrar el vestido y expresar su miedo.

Estar has helped her ask for help, find the dress, and express her fear.

Ahora está en el escenario y empieza su primera frase.

Now she is on stage and begins her first line.

## Querer: The last dinner

Wants, wishes, and affection. Six present-indicative forms; vosotros is used in Spain.

### Guided lesson

En la última cena antes del viaje, querer expresa deseos y también cariño.

At the last dinner before the journey, querer expresses wishes and also affection.

«Quiero irme, pero no quiero haceros daño», dice Lucas.

“I want to leave, but I do not want to hurt you,” Lucas says.

Su hermana pregunta: «¿Quieres viajar o quieres escapar de nosotros?».

His sister asks, “Do you want to travel or do you want to escape from us?”

Su padre interviene: «Él quiere conocer otros lugares».

His father steps in: “He wants to get to know other places.”

«Queremos entenderte, pero también queremos verte», dice su madre.

“We want to understand you, but we also want to see you,” his mother says.

Lucas responde: «Queréis que sea feliz, pero os cuesta dejarme salir».

Lucas replies, “You want me to be happy, but it is hard for you to let me leave.”

Sus abuelos escuchan desde el sofá; quieren hablar sin discutir.

His grandparents listen from the sofa; they want to talk without arguing.

La hermana aparta el plato: «Te quiero y no quiero perderte».

His sister pushes her plate aside: “I love you and I do not want to lose you.”

Lucas se queda callado porque no esperaba escuchar aquello.

Lucas falls silent because he did not expect to hear that.

Después dice: «Si quieres, podemos hablar cada domingo».

Then he says, “If you want, we can talk every Sunday.”

«No queremos una promesa imposible», responde su padre.

“We do not want an impossible promise,” his father replies.

Lucas deja el teléfono y pregunta qué necesita cada uno.

Lucas puts down his phone and asks what each person needs.

### Full-reader continuation

Su madre empieza: «Quiero saber cómo estás, incluso cuando tengas un mal día».

His mother begins, “I want to know how you are, even when you have a bad day.”

Lucas pregunta a su hermana: «¿Quieres enseñarme tus dibujos por videollamada?».

Lucas asks his sister, “Do you want to show me your drawings on a video call?”

Ella asiente; quiere compartir algo más que despedidas.

She nods; she wants to share more than goodbyes.

«Queremos que esta casa siga siendo tu casa», dicen sus padres.

“We want this house to remain your home,” his parents say.

«Y vosotros, ¿qué queréis que os traiga?», pregunta Lucas a sus abuelos.

“And what do you want me to bring you?” Lucas asks his grandparents.

Ellos no quieren regalos; quieren escuchar historias que él haya vivido.

They do not want presents; they want to hear stories he has lived through.

Lucas sonríe: «Quiero aprender a contarlas bien».

Lucas smiles: “I want to learn to tell them well.”

Su hermana se acerca: «Quieres vivir algo nuevo; ahora lo entiendo mejor».

His sister comes closer: “You want to experience something new; now I understand it better.”

La madre quiere una foto antes de que se enfríe la cena.

Their mother wants a photograph before dinner gets cold.

Todos quieren salir juntos en la imagen y acercan las sillas.

Everyone wants to be in the picture together and they move their chairs closer.

Querer no ha eliminado sus diferencias, pero les ha permitido decir qué les importa.

Querer has not removed their differences, but it has allowed them to say what matters to them.

«Os quiero», dice Lucas, y esta vez nadie necesita una explicación.

“I love you,” Lucas says, and this time nobody needs an explanation.
