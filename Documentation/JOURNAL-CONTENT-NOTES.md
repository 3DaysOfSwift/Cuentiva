# Journal collection: sources and editorial decisions

This collection adds 14 original fictional stories, 112 bilingual sentence pairs. The library now contains 34 books: 8 A1, 14 A2, 12 B1. Existing IDs and progress are preserved. Difficulty considers grammar, abstraction, and sentence length; labels and translations still need native-speaker review. The vocabulary index uses curated lemmas with surface-form fallbacks and retains the earlier limitations around ambiguous forms.

The journal is creative input, not an authoritative scientific source. The books preserve its interest in purposeful living without reproducing unsupported factual claims. These are thematic stories and brief book discussions, not comprehensive summaries or authorized adaptations of the named books. They do not claim to establish an author's private intentions.

## Book connections

- [Danny Wallace, Yes Man — publisher page](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/343379/yes-man-by-danny-wallace/9780091896744): the experiment of saying yes supplies a theme of openness. Our fictional learner's boundaries are an editorial addition, not a claim that the book offers that exact prescription.
- [Bob Proctor, You Were Born Rich — official institute page](https://proctorgallagherinstitute.com/tips-tools/you-were-born-rich/): emphasizes potential and personal/financial fulfillment. “Already rich in life and freedom” and the annual planning exercise are treated as the journal writer's interpretation, not a verified complete summary of Proctor's thesis. The story explicitly avoids guaranteed-money or manifestation claims.
- [The Kybalion — original text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14209/pg14209-images.html): presents seven Hermetic principles. We identify this as philosophical/metaphysical writing, not validated modern physics. The story discusses rhythm, polarity, and causation briefly without asserting that its claimed universal laws are scientific results.
- [Harvard: assessment of lectin-free diets](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/lectin-free-diet-more-risky-than-beneficial-experts-say/): used to evaluate the diet themes associated with The Plant Paradox. We have not performed a chapter-by-chapter review of that book, and do not endorse broad elimination of lectin-containing foods.

## Health and nature checks

- [Poison Control: green and sprouted potatoes](https://www.poison.org/articles/are-green-potatoes-safe-to-eat-191): the relevant hazard is elevated glycoalkaloids, especially in green or sprouted potatoes. Ordinary potatoes are not categorically poisonous, and cooking is not a dependable rescue for high-toxin potatoes.
- [MedlinePlus: abdominal sounds](https://www.medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003137.htm): bowel sounds generally reflect normal digestion; a gurgle alone is not evidence of poisoning.
- [Original nicotine measurements in edible nightshades](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10552617/): detected small amounts in foods including tomatoes. Presence alone does not establish meaningful risk from an ordinary serving. The story does not equate eating tomatoes with tobacco exposure.
- [USDA researcher on tomato nutrition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9605204/): identifies tomato as a fruit commonly treated as a vegetable. Botanical fruit develops from a flower's ovary; “anything containing seeds” is not a sufficient universal definition, and seedless cultivated fruits exist. We do not teach that every nut is simply a seed or that all seeds are poisoned.
- [Kew: seed dispersal by animals](https://www.kew.org/read-and-watch/plant-seed-dispersal-animal-poo): some seeds survive digestion and disperse in droppings. This is not a conscious plan by a plant, does not apply to every seed, and does not show that a fruit safe for an animal is safe for humans.
- [Harvard: fruit and vegetable preparation](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/common-questions-fruits-vegetables/): cooking changes nutrients in different ways; it does not simply destroy all nutritional value.
- [WHO: healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet): variety includes fruit, vegetables, pulses, whole grains, and protein sources. Fruit is valuable but not a complete diet, cure for fatigue, or the singular answer to nutrition.
- [WHO: alcohol](https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/alcohol): ethanol is toxic and psychoactive. The “death versus life” comparison is metaphor, not biochemistry; fruit is not an antidote or scientific opposite of alcohol.
- [NIDDK: fasting and diabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/diabetes-discoveries-practice/patients-intermittent-fasting): appropriateness and medication implications depend on the person. The story offers no fasting regimen or claim that regular meals prevent intestinal repair.

The Rockefeller meal-schedule attribution is unverified and omitted. The Warren Buffett car anecdote is not quoted or attributed without a primary source; the story uses an original, general car-care comparison. Evolution informs biology but does not yield one universal ancestral menu; diets and environments have varied. We do not imply that illness reflects poor character or that lifestyle guarantees protection.
